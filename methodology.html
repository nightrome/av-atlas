Summary

This site indexes autonomous vehicle (AV) research papers from major computer vision and robotics venues and reports who is publishing, which institutions and countries are active, how research topics have shifted over time, and which papers are most cited within AV research specifically. Every classification on this site, whether a paper is AV-relevant, which topic it belongs to, who wrote it, or who cites it, is produced by an automated pipeline, not manual review. Figures should be read as a well-informed estimate, not a verified count.

Paper discovery pipeline

Five stages, run roughly in order, with one feedback loop. Discovering who cites an existing paper can surface a title none of the venue listings had yet, which re-enters as a normal entry and is processed the same way as everything else.

  • 1. Collect. Full proceedings from each venue's own source: CVF, DBLP, OpenAlex, the arXiv API. No keyword filter. Every paper from a covered venue and year is included.
  • 2. Merge & classify. Dedupe across sources by normalized title. Assign a category and an AV-relevance label from keyword rules and a language-model second opinion.
  • 3. Enrich. For AV-relevant papers only: author affiliations (from a CVF PDF or an arXiv preprint's HTML rendering), ORCID and Semantic Scholar author identifiers, and profile photos for top-ranked authors.
  • 4. Citation graph. Reference lists parsed from CVF PDFs and arXiv HTML build in-corpus citation edges. A separate query against Semantic Scholar's citation graph finds who else cites a seed paper (the feedback loop above).
  • 5. Aggregate. Leaderboards, per-author/institution/country rollups, and category breakdowns are computed and published to every page on this site.

AV relevance classification & validation

Method

  • Category is a topic label (object detection, tracking, planning and control, etc.) from a living, extensible taxonomy, assigned by matching title and abstract text against each category's keyword list. These keywords are broad computer-vision/robotics terms that also match plenty of non-AV papers once the corpus is full venue proceedings, so category alone is not used to decide AV-relevance.
  • AV-relevance (relevant or not) is decided independently of category. A paper counts as AV-relevant if its title or abstract contains an explicit AV-specific phrase (e.g. "autonomous driving", "self-driving", or a well-known AV dataset name such as nuScenes or KITTI), or if a language model, reading the title and abstract, judges that the paper's technology is directly applied to autonomous vehicles rather than merely being potentially useful for them: a paper that lists "autonomous driving" only as one of several possible downstream applications does not qualify. There is no partial credit: a paper is either AV-relevant or it isn't, and only relevant papers appear in this site's rankings. Papers about vehicle dynamics or chassis control in isolation (mechanical/hardware engineering, no learning-based or software component) are explicitly excluded, even when "vehicle" or "drive" appears in the title.

Validation

65 papers were hand-labeled by a human reviewer (not a language model), a mix of clear AV-relevant papers, clear non-AV papers, and deliberately borderline cases (adjacent computer vision/robotics work that mentions autonomous driving without being about it). This is genuine outside ground truth, not one automated method checked against another. Separately, the language model's judgment alone (not combined with the keyword rule) scores 78.5% accuracy against the same 65 labels, lower, because on its own it misses some genuinely AV-relevant papers that the keyword rule alone catches (67.4% recall by itself vs. 95.1% once combined). This is exactly why the site ORs the two signals together rather than relying on the language model alone.

Citation counts

Every citation count on this site is internal: how many other papers already in this corpus cite a given paper. This is computed entirely from data collected for this site, so it measures something specific (impact within AV research, not research in general) and can be shown and redistributed freely. It is necessarily a much smaller number than a global citation count, since it only counts citations from papers that have themselves been collected here. External citation databases frequently disagree with each other and generally cannot be redistributed, so this site does not use them as a citation source; see Crawler status below for how much of the corpus has had its citations computed so far.

A "no citation data" paper is not necessarily a paper with zero citations. It may be a paper no other paper in this corpus cites yet, or one whose own reference list has not yet been processed. There is no way to tell "genuinely uncited" apart from "not yet processed" from the citation count alone.

Institution / country / researcher data

Author affiliations (institution and country) are looked up separately for AV-relevant papers; coverage is not yet complete for the full corpus (see Crawler status below), and the Institutions/Countries pages always reflect current coverage. Because sample sizes can be small relative to the full corpus, both a total and a per-paper average citation figure are shown for every institution/country/researcher; neither is a substitute for the underlying paper count, which is also always shown.

Affiliation text is sometimes noisy or incorrect on the source's end. Obviously broken entries (bare country/city names, stray addresses, a department name with no parent institution attached, sentence fragments from a failed parse) are filtered out of what is displayed. A generic lab or department name is rejected even when its parent institution is real and appears separately for the same author (e.g. Carnegie Mellon University's Robotics Institute drops the redundant fragment; the author still shows up under Carnegie Mellon University itself). An institution is shown under its highest-level parent (e.g. "University of Oxford", not one of its individual colleges), and known corporate-lab variants are folded into one canonical name (e.g. every Bosch research-lab spelling shows as "Bosch"). This cleanup is ongoing, not exhaustive.

Researchers are matched by name text alone. There is no author disambiguation (no ORCID lookup for most authors, no institution-based clustering). Two different people who happen to share a name are shown as one "researcher" with both people's papers merged together; a person who publishes under slightly different name spellings is shown as two separate researchers.

Network page

Each node is an author (sized by paper count); an edge connects two authors who have co-authored at least one AV-relevant paper in this corpus. Color groups authors into collaboration clusters, found by label propagation over the co-authorship graph itself, not by institution or category, so a cluster is a group who mostly publish with each other, not a group who happen to share an employer. The metrics table's "Centrality" is a weighted PageRank score over that graph: it accounts for multi-hop reach, not just direct collaborator count.

"Cited by" means a paper's own reference list, as parsed from its CVF or arXiv source (the same mechanism behind every paper's Citations panel), names the dataset's paper. It is not a keyword match against an abstract mentioning the dataset's name. Reference-list parsing has not yet reached the full corpus (see Crawler status below), so a low count can mean "not yet processed" as easily as "genuinely unused". Known limitations
  • ICRA and IROS coverage is 2019–2025 only (title/authors, no abstract). Neither conference's own proceedings platform is accessible through any source this site currently uses; 2019–2025 is instead pulled from community-maintained paper lists (see Data sources). 2013–2018 and 2026 aren't covered by any of those and stay missing until a source for those years turns up.
  • RSS, ICLR, AAAI, ICML, and BMVC have no abstracts. Their listing source provides titles and authors but not abstracts, so AV-relevance and category for these venues rest on title text alone, a weaker signal than the rest of the corpus.
  • ICPR, ICASSP, ICIP, ACCV (2013 onward), and ACM Transactions on Graphics are not covered yet. These general ML/CV venues (found by checking which venues this site's own papers cite most often) are queued to be added the same way ICML and BMVC were, but the listing source began rate-limiting mid-fetch and further requests are paused until that clears rather than retried immediately.
  • A meaningful share of papers are labeled "Uncategorized." The topic taxonomy is still being extended to cover them; not all of these are miscategorized, some simply fall outside the current category list.
  • No author disambiguation. Name-only matching merges distinct people who share a name and splits one person across name-spelling variants. See Institution/country/ researcher data above.

Privacy

This site indexes bibliographic metadata (titles, author names, affiliations, venues, years, and citation relationships) about published academic papers. It does not collect any information about visitors to the site.

If you are an author and believe your information is displayed inaccurately, or you would like your name, affiliation, or photo removed or corrected, use the "Report an error on this page" button (🚩) in the navigation bar at the top of any page, or click here. It opens a pre-addressed email to the site's maintainer with the current page's URL included, no account or sign-up is required, and this applies equally to a data correction request and a privacy takedown request.
